Home Warranties

Background

A home warranty is a service contract that covers the repair or replacement of major home systems and appliances due to normal wear and tear. Unlike homeowners insurance, which protects against disasters like fire or theft, a home warranty focuses on the mechanical breakdown of essential home components. Home warranties can be valuable for homebuyers, sellers, and current homeowners, but they also come with limitations that should be carefully considered before purchasing.

What Home Warranties Cover

Home warranty plans typically cover major home systems and appliances, though the extent of coverage depends on the provider and plan selected. Standard home warranty coverage often includes:

Home Systems

  • Heating, ventilation, and air conditioning (HVAC)
  • Electrical systems
  • Plumbing systems
  • Water heaters
  • Ductwork

Major Appliances

  • Refrigerators
  • Dishwashers
  • Ovens and stoves
  • Built-in microwaves
  • Washing machines and dryers
  • Garbage disposals

Optional Add-ons

Some home warranty companies offer additional coverage for:

  • Pools and spas
  • Well pumps
  • Septic systems
  • Roof leaks

Who Can Purchase a Home Warranty?

Home warranties can be purchased at various stages of homeownership:

  • During a real estate transaction – Buyers often purchase a home warranty at closing to protect against unforeseen repairs in their first year of ownership. Sellers may offer one as an incentive to attract buyers.
  • As a new homeowner – If a home was purchased without a warranty, the owner can still buy one at any time.
  • For an existing home – Homeowners who have aging systems and appliances may choose to buy a warranty for financial protection.
  • For rental properties – Landlords can purchase home warranties to help manage maintenance costs.

Benefits of a Home Warranty

  • Financial Protection – A home warranty helps cover costly repairs and replacements, reducing unexpected expenses.
  • Convenience – The warranty provider arranges for service professionals, eliminating the need for homeowners to find their own repair technicians.
  • Increased Buyer Confidence – Home sellers offering a warranty may make their property more appealing by giving buyers peace of mind.
  • Budgeting Assistance – Instead of large, unpredictable repair costs, homeowners pay a predictable annual or monthly fee.

Limitations of a Home Warranty

  • Not Everything is Covered – Most warranties do not cover pre-existing conditions, improper installation, or issues related to poor maintenance.
  • Service Fees Apply – Homeowners must pay a service fee (typically $75 to $150) per claim.
  • Limited Contractor Choice – Warranty companies assign service providers, which may result in slower response times or lower-quality repairs.
  • Claim Denials – If a provider determines that an item failed due to neglect, lack of maintenance, or an excluded issue, they may deny the claim.

Cost and Duration of a Home Warranty

Cost – Home warranties typically range from $300 to $800 per year, depending on the provider and level of coverage. Comprehensive plans or additional coverage options increase the price.

Duration – Most home warranties last one year but can be renewed annually. Some companies offer multi-year plans at discounted rates.

Final Thoughts

A home warranty can be a worthwhile investment for homeowners looking to protect against costly repairs, especially in older homes or during the first year after purchasing a property. However, it's essential to review the contract carefully, understand limitations, and compare providers to determine if a home warranty is the right choice.
