Surveys

Background

A residential property survey is a professional assessment that maps out the legal boundaries of a property. It identifies land features, structures, and any encroachments or easements that may affect ownership rights. These surveys can be beneficial in real estate transactions, helping buyers, sellers, and lenders understand property lines and potential legal issues before closing. Most home sale transactions do not include a survey, however.

Who Can Order a Property Survey?

  • Home Buyers – Buyers often order a survey before closing to confirm property boundaries and ensure there are no disputes.
  • Home Sellers – Sellers may obtain a survey to provide prospective buyers with accurate boundary information.
  • Lenders and Title Companies – Mortgage lenders and title companies sometimes require a survey to verify that no encroachments or legal issues could affect property value.
  • Homeowners – Property owners can order a survey before building fences, driveways, or home additions to avoid encroaching on a neighbor's land.

Why Property Surveys May Be Needed

  1. Confirming Property Boundaries – Ensures the buyer gets exactly what they are paying for and prevents boundary disputes.
  2. Identifying Encroachments – Detects if a neighbor's fence, driveway, or structure extends onto the property.
  3. Verifying Easements – Determines if utility companies or neighbors have legal access to part of the property.
  4. Securing Title Insurance – Some title companies require a survey to issue a title insurance policy.
  5. Facilitating Construction or Renovations – Ensures new structures are placed within property limits and comply with zoning laws.
  6. Settling Boundary Disputes – Helps resolve legal conflicts between neighbors regarding property lines.

Cost of a Property Survey

The cost of a residential property survey varies based on location, property size, and terrain complexity. On average, homeowners can expect to pay:

  • $300 to $800 for a standard residential survey.
  • Up to $1,500 or more for larger or irregularly shaped properties.

Format and What a Survey Covers

A property survey is presented as a detailed map or plat showing:

  • Property boundaries with exact measurements.
  • Structures and improvements (house, garage, sheds, driveways).
  • Easements and right-of-ways (such as utility access areas).
  • Encroachments from neighboring properties.
  • Zoning classification and flood zones (if applicable).

What Property Surveys Do Not Cover

  • Soil quality and environmental hazards – Separate inspections are needed for issues like contamination or unstable ground.
  • Subsurface utilities – Surveys may not show underground lines unless specifically requested.
  • Unrecorded property claims – A survey cannot confirm verbal agreements or unregistered easements.

Limitations and Accuracy Concerns

Older properties may have poorly recorded markers or missing ground pins, making boundary determination challenging. Over time, property features can shift, and historical surveys may be inaccurate. In such cases, surveyors may need to use neighboring properties and historical records to reconstruct boundaries, increasing complexity and cost.

Role in Settling Disputes

Property surveys provide legally recognized boundary data that can resolve disputes between neighbors, title companies, or local governments. Courts often use survey maps in litigation involving property encroachments, easements, or ownership claims.

Conclusion

A property survey is a vital tool for buyers, sellers, and homeowners, ensuring clear boundary lines and preventing legal conflicts. While costs vary, the investment can save thousands in legal fees or property disputes. Whether purchasing a home, refinancing, or planning a construction project, obtaining an updated survey can provide peace of mind and legal protection.
