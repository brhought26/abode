Closing Attorneys & Agents

Background

Real estate transactions involve numerous legal and financial complexities that require expert oversight to ensure a smooth and legally sound closing process. Two key players in this process are closing attorneys and title companies. Both serve essential roles in verifying the legality of property transfers, ensuring a clear title, and protecting the interests of buyers, sellers, and lenders. While they share some overlapping responsibilities, their functions and legal authority differ in significant ways. Depending on your jurisdiction, you will be working with one or the other. Consult with your lender for some recommendations.

The Role of a Closing Attorney

A closing attorney is a licensed legal professional who specializes in real estate law and facilitates the legal aspects of a property transaction. In many states, the involvement of a closing attorney is required by law. Their primary responsibilities include:

  1. Reviewing and Preparing Legal Documents – The closing attorney ensures that all necessary legal documents, such as the purchase agreement, deed, promissory note, and mortgage, are properly drafted and executed and then recorded with the County.
  2. Title Examination – The attorney conducts a thorough title search to verify that the property has a clear title, free from liens, encumbrances, or disputes that could affect ownership rights.
  3. Handling Funds – Closing attorneys oversee the financial aspects of the transaction, ensuring that all payments, including the down payment, lender funds, and closing costs, are properly disbursed.
  4. Ensuring Legal Compliance – They ensure that the transaction adheres to local, state, and federal laws and that all necessary disclosures and requirements are met.
  5. Conducting the Closing – The closing attorney presides over the final signing of documents, explaining the legal implications to all parties involved. They ensure that the property transfer is legally binding.
  6. Recording the Deed – Once the transaction is complete, the attorney ensures that the new deed and any other necessary documents are recorded with the appropriate county office.

The Role of a Title Company

A title company focuses primarily on the title aspect of the transaction. Their role is crucial in ensuring that the property title is legitimate and that the buyer receives ownership without legal complications. The main responsibilities of a title company include:

  1. Conducting a Title Search – The title company performs an extensive search of public records to confirm that the seller has a clear and marketable title.
  2. Issuing Title Insurance – To protect buyers and lenders from potential future claims or title defects, title companies issue title insurance policies. These policies provide financial protection against undiscovered title issues.
  3. Acting as an Escrow Agent – In many cases, title companies serve as escrow agents, holding funds in escrow until all transaction conditions are met.
  4. Facilitating Closing Logistics – While title companies do not provide legal advice, they often help coordinate the signing of documents and disbursement of funds to the appropriate parties.

Key Differences Between Closing Attorneys and Title Companies

  1. Legal Authority – Closing attorneys are licensed legal professionals who can provide legal advice and represent clients in case of disputes. Title companies do not practice law and cannot provide legal counsel.
  2. Geographical Requirements – Some states, such as Georgia, South Carolina, and Massachusetts, require a closing attorney to handle real estate transactions, while other states allow title companies to conduct closings without an attorney.
  3. Scope of Services – Closing attorneys offer a broader range of legal services beyond title examination, including contract review, legal compliance, and dispute resolution. Title companies focus primarily on title searches and insurance.
  4. Cost Considerations – The cost of hiring a closing attorney may be higher than using a title company alone, but it provides additional legal protection and expertise.

Conclusion

Both closing attorneys and title companies play vital roles in real estate transactions. While title companies ensure that the property title is clear and issue insurance to protect against potential title defects, closing attorneys provide legal oversight to ensure that the entire transaction is legally compliant and properly executed. Depending on state laws and the complexity of a transaction, one or both may be required.
